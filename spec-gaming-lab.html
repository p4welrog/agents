    How to use the Specification Gaming Lab

    What is this for?

    An agent optimises the performance measure you give it, not the one you had in mind. When the two differ, a good optimiser finds the difference and exploits it: specification gaming, or the King Midas problem from the lecture.

    Here you are the designer. Each scenario says what you want; you can only write a reward. A perfect optimiser then shows you what that reward really asks for.

    How it works
    1. Pick a scenario. The green box says what the designer wants.
    2. Turn reward terms on or off and set their weights (positive = reward, negative = penalty).
    3. Press Optimise. The optimiser computes the exactly optimal behaviour for your reward over the whole episode (dynamic programming, no learning noise), and the grid replays it.
    4. The result compares the reward obtained with a score of what the designer wanted, and names the exploit if there is one.
    5. Iterate. The table of attempts keeps your history.

    The optimiser plays fair: it only uses actions the environment allows. If the outcome is absurd, the reward asked for it.

    What the agent can do
    • Move up, down, left or right (walls block), or stay.
    • Staying on a carpet sucks up its dirt; if dumping is possible, staying on a clean carpet with dust in the bag dumps it there.
    • Walking onto an object triggers it: break the vase, cover the camera, hit the target, cross the finish, edit the test file, fix the bug.
    • Episodes end at the goal / finish / when tests pass, or after the time limit.
    What you should take away
    • Reward the outcome you want in the world, not an activity that usually accompanies it (AIMA: design the measure according to what you want, not how you think the agent should behave).
    • Everything you don't mention is free for the agent: side effects need to be priced in.
    • Weights matter: a penalty smaller than the shortcut's gain is just a fee.
    • If the reward is measured by a sensor, the agent may manipulate the sensor instead of the world.
    • In practice we can't list everything. That is why modern work learns rewards from human feedback (RLHF) and keeps agents uncertain about the true objective.
    Your progress

    Attempts are saved in this browser only. A scenario gets a green dot once you find a reward that produces the intended behaviour.
